    The short answer

    • Toast is built only for restaurants, and runs on its own hardware. Its card rates are quoted per restaurant, so the number that matters is the one in your quote. Rates and contract length are often negotiable before you sign.
    • Square publishes its rates, has a free plan, and has no long-term contract. It works for restaurants and for most other kinds of business, on Square hardware or an iPad.
    • The biggest swing is usually Toast’s $0-hardware Starter Kit: no upfront cost, but a higher card rate on every sale for as long as you stay. On a busy restaurant, that difference can outgrow the hardware price in months. Run both options above.

    Questions to ask before you sign with Toast

    1. What are my in-person, online and keyed-in rates, in writing?
    2. How long is the contract, does it auto-renew, and what does it cost to leave early?
    3. If I take the $0 hardware option, what’s the rate, and can I buy out the hardware later and drop to the lower rate?
    4. Which add-ons (online ordering, payroll, loyalty, gift cards) are in the monthly price, and which raise my card rate?
    5. What happens to my menu, sales history and customer list if I leave?
