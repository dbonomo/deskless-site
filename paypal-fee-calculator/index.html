    How to receive an exact amount

    The fee comes out of what the customer pays, so adding the fee to your price isn’t quite enough: the fee on the added amount is still missing. The right formula is (amount you want + fixed fee) / (1 − rate). For a PayPal button payment, to receive $100 you charge (100 + 0.49) / (1 − 0.0349) = $104.13. The calculator does this for every payment type.

    Check your state’s rules and PayPal’s terms before adding a fee to a customer’s bill. Surcharging rules differ by state and by card network.

    When PayPal is worth it, and when it isn’t

    • Worth it: customers who trust the PayPal button, or who’d rather not type a card number into a small business’s site. For some online stores that alone lifts sales enough to cover the extra fee.
    • Worth a second look: invoices for larger jobs. A $2,000 invoice paid with the PayPal button costs $70.29; paid by bank transfer through PayPal, $10. Contractors and service businesses can save real money by nudging customers to the bank option.
    • Watch out: Pay Later at 4.99% on everything, and international customers at an extra 1.5% plus currency conversion.
