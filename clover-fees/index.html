    Clover’s price depends on who sold it to you

    Clover is owned by Fiserv and sold two ways: directly on clover.com, and through thousands of banks and resellers. The prices on Clover’s site apply only to clover.com purchases; Clover says contracts and termination fees “vary based on your service provider.” If you got Clover from your bank or a sales rep, your statement, not Clover’s website, is the truth.

    One example of how different it can be: Wells Fargo’s published Clover pricing is 2.60% + 15¢ in person and 3.50% + 15¢ online or keyed for businesses under $40,000 a month, plus a $9.95 monthly merchant account fee and a $10 monthly PCI fee. Wells Fargo says it has no early termination fee.

    The fine print worth reading twice

    • Hardware subscriptions can’t be cancelled. Clover’s FAQ calls them “non-cancelable and ineligible for refunds.” At the end of 36 months they roll to month-to-month unless you give 30 days’ notice.
    • Promotions come with a contract. Clover’s $450 statement credit requires a 3-year contract (offer ends December 31, 2026).
    • Early termination fees aren’t published. They depend on the provider. Ask for the exact amount in writing before you sign anything.
    • Fast funding costs extra. Clover Rapid Deposit moves money the same day for 1.75% of the amount. Clover describes standard settlement as taking 1 to 3 days.
    • The hardware stays with the processor. Clover devices bought through one provider generally can’t be moved to another processor.

    Questions to ask before you sign

    1. Who is my processor: Clover directly, a bank, or a reseller?
    2. What are my rates for in person, online and keyed, and are there monthly account, PCI or statement fees?
    3. How long is the contract, and what exactly does it cost to leave early?
    4. Is the hardware bought, leased or subscribed, and can I cancel it?
    5. When does money reach my bank, and what does faster funding cost?
