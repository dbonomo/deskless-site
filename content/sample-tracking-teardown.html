Conversion-tracking teardown: three domains, two ad platforms, one CRM

What a client receives. This sample merges findings from two real pre-sale teardowns (a tutoring company and a regional transportation business); names, domains and IDs removed. Scope: main site, a booking page, an SEO blog subdomain, and a customer portal on a separate top-level domain. Done from the public sites only — no account access.

Summary

1 · The blog is invisible to GA4 and Google Ads: a consent plugin defaults analytics_storage to denied on a US site with no consent banner, so no visitor can ever opt in.

2 · Meta has no conversion signal at all: the pixel fires on the homepage and never loads on the booking page, so every Meta-sourced lead is untracked.

3 · Cross-domain sessions break at the portal: no GA4 linker, the portal hostname is missing from the tag-manager lookup table, and three GA4 properties split the funnel — conversions land as Direct in a property that never saw the ad click.

What the client thought was wrong vs. what was

  • Known to client: the missing cross-domain linker (03) and lost gclids (06). Real, but not the biggest.
  • Unknown to client: the silent consent-mode default (02), the pixel missing on the booking page (01), the lookup-table gap (04), the duplicate property (09). Two of these affect more spend than the one they asked about.

Fix order · what I'd do first

  • Day 1: consent defaults on the blog (02) and pixel on the booking page (01). Two config changes; both restore signal immediately.
  • Day 2: one property decision, then linker + lookup table together (03, 04, 05). Fixing the linker alone does not fix attribution.
  • Day 3–4: click-ID hidden fields (06), CRM script everywhere (10), pixel rationalization (08), cleanup (07, 09, 11, 12).
  • Day 5: end-to-end verification — Google ad click → landing → booking → GA4 conversion + Ads conversion + Meta Lead + CRM contact with correct source. Same for a Meta click. Documented, with every tag and container ID.

Public-site inspection of the live tag-manager container scripts, direct gtag installs, consent calls, form markup, and outbound-link decoration. No account access was needed for any finding above. Read-only ad-account access is only used when the client wants spend figures attached to each row.
