None of this is on the card. It’s what tends to happen once the first piece of work is good.

  • Sometimes the honest answer after 90 minutes is “we don’t know what’s true yet.” Then the next step is a week of me checking the system your decisions ride on, from the outside in, and writing down what’s true, what’s broken, and what each broken thing is costing you — ranked, with a fix for each one.
  • The Adobe Analytics or GA4 data you’re moving into Snowflake, BigQuery or Fabric — export design, the eVar/prop → XDM → warehouse-table translation, dbt models your BI team can actually use.
  • The reporting setup that quietly disagrees with itself — traced back to the source and fixed there, not papered over in the dashboard.
  • The internal tool that’s been “next quarter” for two years.
  • A pile of documents nobody can read — tens of thousands of reports, surveys, PDFs — turned into a dataset you can query and a report you can stand behind. (I’ve done this for the UN.)

If one of those is you, start with the 90 minutes anyway. It’s cheaper for both of us to find out on $500 whether we work well together.

04 Frequently asked

Frequently asked

Q1 What do you need access to? +

For the 90 minutes, nothing but a screen you’re willing to share. For a fix or a build, the least access that does the job — read-only wherever read-only works, exported snapshots where those are enough, and no change to production that isn’t in writing first. NDA on request, and I delete what you send me on delivery.

Q2 Can I see what I’d get before paying? +

Yes. The sample findings report and the anonymized finding are both linked above. Same format, same depth, your data instead of invented data.

Q3 Who actually does the work? +

I do. There is no bench and no junior behind the curtain. Automated checks run first, then I read every finding and write every fix myself.

Q4 Does the $500 come off a bigger project? +

Yes — credited in full against any project you book. And if the answer after 90 minutes is “this isn’t worth paying for,” you get that answer in writing and we both get on with our week.

Q5 Is this a retainer? +

No. Every offer is a fixed price with a written scope and an end. If you want me again later, it gets quoted again — and by then I already know where the bodies are buried.

Q6 What if the job turns out to be bigger than the box? +

You find out on day one, in writing, and you can stop at no charge. If we go ahead, the price doesn’t move — going over the cap is my problem, which is the whole point of a fixed price.

Q7 Why hours instead of weeks? +

Because I have a day job and “two weeks” would be a promise about my calendar rather than your work. You buy a capped number of my hours — 22 or 45 — and I quote a start date and a finish date alongside the scope. Fewer impressive adjectives, more keeping my word.

Q8 Will this eat my team’s time? +

The 90 minutes needs you and a screen. A fix or a build needs one person who can answer questions and grant access — not a working group.

Q9 Is the team day in person? +

In person anywhere on the Front Range, or remote if that’s easier. Up to eight people, on your tools and your real work. No slides, no certificate, no trust falls.

Q10 Do you work locally? +

Yes — Fort Collins and the Front Range, in person when it helps. Most of the work is remote and asynchronous, and calls happen during US business hours, Mountain or Central.

Q11 Why “Deskless”? +

Because the point is technology that works for the people doing the work, not more dashboards for the people watching them. It’s the line in the footer, and I did mean it.

Ninety minutes, $500, one problem. You get a written plan and a straight answer, including the free one: don’t pay anyone for this.
