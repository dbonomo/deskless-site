    How to read your number

    Your effective rate is total fees divided by card sales. It’s the only number that lets you compare one processor with another, because it includes everything: the rate, the per-sale fee, and the monthly charges that don’t show up in a sales pitch.

    • Close to the flat-rate options or below them: you’re in good shape. Switching would save little and cost time.
    • A few tenths of a percent above: worth a phone call. Ask your processor to remove monthly add-on fees or match a competitor’s rate. That call costs nothing and sometimes works.
    • A full point or more above: something is wrong. Usually it’s monthly fees on low volume, old tiered pricing, or a rate that crept up after a promotional period.

    The three ways processors price

    Flat rate

    One rate for every card of a given type: Square, Stripe and Clover’s published plans work this way. Easy to read and predict. You pay the same on a cheap debit card as on a premium rewards card, so the processor keeps more on the cheap ones.

    Interchange-plus

    The processor passes through what the card networks and issuing banks charge (interchange), which differs by card type, then adds a fixed markup, such as a fraction of a percent plus a few cents. At higher volume this is often the cheapest honest option. Only the markup is negotiable, and the statements are long.

    Tiered

    Sales are sorted into “qualified”, “mid-qualified” and “non-qualified” buckets, each with its own rate. The processor decides what goes in which bucket. The low “qualified” rate is what gets quoted; many of your sales land in the higher ones. If your statement uses these words, run the calculator.

    Fees worth looking for on a statement

    • Monthly minimum: if your processing fees fall below a set amount, you pay the difference.
    • PCI non-compliance fee: charged monthly until you complete a yearly security questionnaire. Often fixable in an afternoon.
    • Statement, account or “regulatory” fees: small monthly lines that add up.
    • Equipment lease: leased terminals can cost far more over the lease than buying one outright, and the lease is often non-cancellable.
    • Early termination fee: check your contract before switching; sometimes the saving pays it off in a few months, sometimes it doesn’t.

    Before you switch

    Fees aren’t the whole decision. Your point-of-sale system, hardware, payout speed, who answers the phone, and how your sales data connects to your books matter too. Moving processors usually means changing hardware and retraining staff. If the yearly saving in the calculator is small, the call to your current processor is the better move.
